# Engineering Units Reference

Engineering uses a wide range of measurement units for describing physical quantities such as force, pressure, energy, power, stress, torque, flow, and temperature.

This reference provides a concise overview of commonly used engineering quantities and their SI units.

## SI Base Units Used in Engineering

| Quantity            | SI Unit  | Symbol |
| ------------------- | -------- | ------ |
| Length              | meter    | m      |
| Mass                | kilogram | kg     |
| Time                | second   | s      |
| Electric current    | ampere   | A      |
| Temperature         | kelvin   | K      |
| Amount of substance | mole     | mol    |

## Force

The SI unit of force is the newton (N).

**1 N = 1 kg·m/s²**

Commonly encountered force units include:

* newton (N)
* kilonewton (kN)
* pound-force (lbf)

## Pressure

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa).

**1 Pa = 1 N/m²**

Common pressure units include:

* pascal (Pa)
* kilopascal (kPa)
* megapascal (MPa)
* bar
* atmosphere (atm)
* pounds per square inch (psi)

## Energy

The SI unit of energy is the joule (J).

Common energy units include:

* joule (J)
* kilojoule (kJ)
* megajoule (MJ)
* watt-hour (Wh)
* kilowatt-hour (kWh)

## Power

The SI unit of power is the watt (W).

**1 W = 1 J/s**

Common power units include:

* watt (W)
* kilowatt (kW)
* megawatt (MW)
* horsepower (hp)

## Torque

Torque is commonly expressed in newton-meters (N·m).

**Torque = Force × Perpendicular Distance**

The SI unit is:

**N·m**

Torque should not be confused with energy, even though both can have the same dimensional units.

## Density

The SI unit of density is kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³).

**Density = Mass / Volume**

Common engineering density units include:

* kg/m³
* g/cm³
* kg/L

## Flow Rate

Volumetric flow rate describes the volume of fluid passing through a given point per unit time.

The SI unit is:

**m³/s**

Other commonly used units include:

* L/s
* L/min
* m³/h
* ft³/s

## Temperature

Engineering applications commonly use:

* kelvin (K)
* degree Celsius (°C)
* degree Fahrenheit (°F)

Kelvin is the SI base unit for thermodynamic temperature.

## Dimensional Consistency

Engineering calculations should maintain dimensional consistency. Quantities in an equation must have compatible dimensions, and unit conversions should be performed before combining values when necessary.

For example:

**Force = Mass × Acceleration**

Therefore:

**N = kg·m/s²**

Maintaining consistent units helps reduce calculation errors and makes engineering calculations easier to audit.
