# Measurement Systems

A measurement system is a standardized way of expressing physical quantities using defined units. Different systems have been used throughout history, while the International System of Units (SI) is now the primary system used in science, engineering, and international measurement.

## International System of Units (SI)

The International System of Units (SI) is the modern metric system. It is based on seven base units from which many other units are derived.

| Physical Quantity         | SI Base Unit | Symbol |
| ------------------------- | ------------ | ------ |
| Length                    | meter        | m      |
| Mass                      | kilogram     | kg     |
| Time                      | second       | s      |
| Electric current          | ampere       | A      |
| Thermodynamic temperature | kelvin       | K      |
| Amount of substance       | mole         | mol    |
| Luminous intensity        | candela      | cd     |

SI also includes derived units such as:

* newton (N) — force
* pascal (Pa) — pressure
* joule (J) — energy
* watt (W) — power
* coulomb (C) — electric charge
* volt (V) — electric potential difference
* ohm (Ω) — electrical resistance
* hertz (Hz) — frequency

## Metric System

The metric system is based on powers of ten. It uses decimal prefixes to represent multiples and submultiples of units.

Examples include:

* kilometer (km)
* centimeter (cm)
* millimeter (mm)
* milligram (mg)
* megawatt (MW)

The modern SI system developed from the metric system and provides internationally standardized definitions and relationships between units.

## CGS System

The centimetre-gram-second (CGS) system uses:

* centimetre (cm) for length
* gram (g) for mass
* second (s) for time

Some CGS-derived units are still encountered in specialized scientific literature.

## Imperial and US Customary Units

Imperial and US customary units are non-SI measurement systems used in different contexts.

Common examples include:

* inch (in)
* foot (ft)
* yard (yd)
* mile (mi)
* ounce (oz)
* pound (lb)
* gallon (gal)

Imperial and US customary units are not identical in every case. For example, the Imperial gallon and US gallon have different volumes.

## SI and Non-SI Units

SI units are commonly preferred in scientific and engineering work because they provide a consistent international framework.

However, non-SI units continue to be used in areas such as construction, transportation, cooking, trade, and everyday measurements.

Reliable conversion factors are therefore important when working between different measurement systems.

## Choosing a Measurement System

The appropriate unit system depends on the application, location, industry, and technical requirements.

When performing calculations:

1. Identify the units of every input.
2. Convert quantities to compatible units when necessary.
3. Perform the calculation.
4. Express the result using an appropriate unit.
5. Check dimensional consistency.

## Unit Conversion

Converting between measurement systems requires accurate conversion factors.

For example:

**1 inch = 0.0254 meter**

**1 foot = 0.3048 meter**

**1 mile = 1.609344 kilometers**
